# Hashwork: Vision & Market Clarity Framework
## What Are We Actually Building? And For Whom?

## Part 1: Define the Vision (Choose One, Or Clarify Hybrid)

### Vision A: Technical Validation Operating System
**Core Thesis:** Presales is a process, not a role. It has stages (discovery → qualification → POC → validation → handoff). No one manages this process end-to-end today.

**Problem it solves:** 
- Technical validation activities are scattered across tools (email, Slack, spreadsheet, Gong, Salesforce notes)
- No clear process or accountability for moving deals through technical stages
- Deals slip through cracks (legal approved, but POC still in progress; sales ready to close, but security review not done)

**Market size:** Every B2B SaaS company with 3+ SEs and deals >$100K

**Why no one owns it:**
- CRMs focus on sales stages (demo → proposal → negotiation)
- Presales tools focus on specific activities (demos, calls, operations metrics)
- No one manages the entire technical validation workflow

**Competitor comparison:**
- **Vivun:** Measures SE effort and outputs
- **Gong:** Records and analyzes conversations
- **Navattic:** Automates first-touch demos
- **Hashwork could be:** The workflow engine that orchestrates technical validation

---

### Vision B: Deal Intelligence (Predict Close Probability Based on Technical Validation)
**Core Thesis:** Sales reps think they know which deals will close. They're wrong. Technical validation signals predict close probability better than rep confidence.

**Problem it solves:**
- Reps are optimistic (forecasts are always wrong on the optimistic side)
- Deal stage doesn't predict close (you can be in "negotiation" but technical validation is blocked)
- Technical risks emerge late and kill deals (should be caught at POC stage, not at legal review)

**Market size:** Every sales leader managing >$10M pipeline who wants forecast accuracy

**Why no one does this well:**
- Gong predicts probability, but from conversation signals (what people say), not technical validation (what actually works)
- Vivun tracks SE effort, not technical progress
- CRMs assume stage = progress (wrong)

**Competitor comparison:**
- **Gong:** Probability from conversation signals (50% from calls, 50% from activity)
- **Hashwork could be:** Probability from technical validation signals (is the architecture sound? Have security issues been resolved? Will it perform at their peak load?)

---

### Vision C: SE Productivity & Allocation
**Core Thesis:** SEs are the scarcest resource in enterprise software sales. Most companies don't know if they're allocating them well.

**Problem it solves:**
- SEs spend time on low-impact work (repetitive demos that could be automated)
- High-value SEs are overallocated (3 POCs when 2 would be better)
- Presales managers can't see: Who is busy? On what? Is it working?
- No feedback loop from "SE spent 20 hours on Acme deal" → "Acme closed $500K" → "that 20 hours generated $25K value"

**Market size:** Every presales leader managing >5 SEs

**Why no one does this:**
- Vivun captures some of this, but macro-level (total SE effort vs. total deals closed)
- No one does deal-by-deal SE ROI tracking
- SEs don't log time (it's tedious, they won't do it)

**Competitor comparison:**
- **Vivun:** "Average SE ROI is $X per hour"
- **Hashwork could be:** "Sarah on Acme POC: 20 hours, $500K deal, $25K ROI. Marcus on NextGen POC: 40 hours, $180K deal, $4.5K ROI. Sarah is 5x more efficient. Allocate her to bigger deals."

---

### Vision D: Technical Risk Prediction & Mitigation
**Core Thesis:** 50-70% of deals fail not because of budget/competition, but because of technical incompatibility discovered too late.

**Problem it solves:**
- Deals move through sales stages (demo → POC → negotiation) before technical fit is proven
- Technical issues (incompatible architecture, performance requirements, integration gaps) discovered at negotiation kill deals that could have been won with different approach
- SEs discover problems reactively, not proactively

**Market size:** Enterprise software companies (SaaS, infrastructure, databases, DevOps)

**Why no one does this:**
- Technical validation is domain-specific (every type of software has different risks)
- Requires domain expertise + process design
- No one has built a repeatable framework for technical qualification (like MEDDIC for sales)

**Competitor comparison:**
- **CRMs:** No technical risk tracking
- **Vivun/Gong:** No technical risk prediction
- **Hashwork could be:** "Based on their architecture, here are 3 technical risks: (1) API rate limits, (2) Data volume handling, (3) Legacy system integration. Probability of issue: 40%. Likely to kill deal: Yes. Mitigation: POC should include load testing."

---

### Vision E: Deal Cycle Acceleration
**Core Thesis:** Presales is the bottleneck in modern B2B sales. You can't close faster without presales going faster.

**Problem it solves:**
- Sales cycles are 6-12 months (57% say getting longer)
- Technical validation takes 40-60% of cycle time
- Most of that time is wasted: waiting for customer, environment setup, redundant discovery, rework
- Could be cut 20-40% with better process

**Market size:** Every company selling enterprise software

**Why no one does this:**
- Requires operational excellence (process design, automation, discipline)
- Most presales teams are ad-hoc
- Haven't been forced to optimize yet (still growing)

**Competitor comparison:**
- No one owns this end-to-end
- Could integrate everything: demos (automated), discovery (scripted), POC (templates), handoff (playbooks)

## Part 2: Market Reality for Each Vision

### Market Size & Growth

**General presales market:**
- No specific "presales operations" market size (not tracked separately)
- Embedded in: sales enablement ($25B+ market), CRM ($70B+ market), deal management
- Growing fast because: Sales cycles getting longer (57%), more stakeholders (6-10), compliance complexity (61% blocked by security checks)

**Market structure:**
- **Horizontal tools:** CRM (Salesforce, HubSpot), call intel (Gong), demos (Navattic, Naoma)
- **Vertical specialists:** Vivun (presales ops), Rocketlane (PSA), industry-specific (legal tech, healthcare tech, etc.)
- **No category leader** for "presales operating system" — market is fragmented

**Price expectations:**
- Demo tools: $50-300/month per user
- Call intel: $1,400-2,000/seat/year
- Presales ops (Vivun): Custom pricing ($50K-500K+ for enterprise)
- CRM: $100-300/seat/month

## Part 3: Competitive Positioning by Vision

### If Vision A (Technical Validation OS):
**Direct competitors:** Vivun, Homerun
**Positioning:** "We own the technical validation process. Vivun tracks SE effort; we manage the workflow."
**Strengths:** Deep workflow design, could integrate demos + calls + collaboration
**Risks:** Vivun is entrenched, better funded, already integrated

### If Vision B (Deal Intelligence):
**Direct competitors:** Gong, Clari
**Positioning:** "Probability scoring based on technical signals, not conversation signals."
**Strengths:** Unique signal source (technical validation progress)
**Risks:** Gong is category leader, much larger, has better data

### If Vision C (SE Productivity):
**Direct competitors:** Vivun, Kantata (PSA tools adapted for presales)
**Positioning:** "SE allocation and ROI tracking that actually works because we auto-track time."
**Strengths:** New market (no one does this well), addresses real pain (SEs are precious)
**Risks:** Presales leaders are small TAM, need high MRR per customer

### If Vision D (Technical Risk):
**Direct competitors:** None (this is a gap)
**Positioning:** "Predict which deals will fail on technical fit before you waste sales cycle time."
**Strengths:** Unique problem, high-value if accurate
**Risks:** Domain-specific, requires deep vertical expertise, hard to scale

### If Vision E (Cycle Acceleration):
**Direct competitors:** Everyone and no one (it's about optimization)
**Positioning:** "Cut 20-40% off deal cycles by automating presales workflow."
**Strengths:** Everyone wants faster cycles, resonates with all stakeholders
**Risks:** Requires operational excellence, hard to prove ROI, depends on other tools working well

## Part 6: The Customer Interview Questions

To clarify which vision matters most, ask presales leaders:

1. **On process:** "How do you define the stages of technical validation in your deals? Is it consistent, or ad-hoc?"

2. **On prediction:** "When you look at your closed-won and closed-lost deals, what predicted the outcome? (Sales stage? Rep activity? Technical fit? Budget? Competitive loss?)"

3. **On efficiency:** "What's the longest and shortest technical validation took? Why the variance? Could you cut it 20%?"

4. **On allocation:** "How do you decide which SE works on which deal? Do you track whether that decision was good?"

5. **On risk:** "What percentage of deals failed for technical reasons (not sales reasons)? How early could you have detected those technical issues?"

6. **On tools:** "You use [Salesforce + Gong + Vivun + spreadsheet]. Why not consolidate? What's missing from each?"

7. **On pain:** "What keeps you up at night about presales?" (This is the real one.)

## Part 7: Market Reality Check

**What the market is telling us:**
- Sales cycles are getting LONGER (57% of reps say this)
- Deals are getting MORE COMPLEX (6-10 stakeholders per deal)
- Technical validation is becoming MORE CRITICAL (security/compliance blocks 61% of deals)
- SEs are MORE SCARCE (talent shortage across technical roles)
- Tools are MORE FRAGMENTED (no single tool wins, teams stack 2-3 tools)

**This suggests:**
- There's room for a new platform (market is moving, tools aren't keeping up)
- It should be focused (specialize, don't try to replace CRM)
- It should integrate (complement existing tools, don't compete)
- It should target efficiency (SEs are expensive, cutting cycle time saves massive costs)

## Part 8: What the Vision Determines

Once you clarify the vision, it determines:

**Product roadmap:** What features matter, what's secondary
**Competitive strategy:** How to position vs. Vivun/Gong/CRMs
**GTM strategy:** Who to sell to, how to get first customer
**Pricing:** How much value are you creating? What can you charge?
**Hiring:** What expertise do you need? Engineers vs. product vs. sales

**You can't choose all five visions. Pick one (or a clear hybrid), and build that.**
